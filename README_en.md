## What This Book Is About

In the splendid manor of Victorian-era Britain, young Winston Churchill should have been the darling of fate — a privileged life, aristocratic bloodline, everything like a crystal fairy tale. Yet reality was far from it. In his autobiography, Churchill candidly wrote that despite being born into a prestigious noble family, his emotional world was a barren wasteland.

His parents were distant from him. His father, Lord Randolph, a brilliant yet erratic politician, always looked at his "clumsy" son with disappointment, dissatisfied with his academic performance, his words full of belittlement and harshness. His mother Jennie, though beautiful and socially active, rarely took time to offer emotional comfort. Churchill once described her: "She shone for me like the Evening Star. I loved her dearly — but at a distance."

As an adult, Churchill led Britain against the Nazis as Prime Minister. During one exchange, his political opponent Lady Astor fired a venomous arrow: "If I were your wife, I would put poison in your coffee." The air seemed to freeze, everyone holding their breath. Churchill smiled slightly and replied wittily: "If I were your husband, I would drink that cup of coffee." This not only defused the political awkwardness but also revealed his unique way of using humor to mask his wounds — that slight self-mockery in the face of life's adversity, perhaps stemming from the emotional desolation of his childhood.

Churchill's greatness lies not in never having been hurt, but in his lifelong courage to face the "black dog" (his name for his depression) — he spent half his life buried in books, reading widely, trying to heal his childhood trauma and transcend his own limitations.

In 1953, he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature, a testament not only to his extraordinary perseverance but also to the defense of noble human values in his writing.

Not all of us can spend a lifetime learning to heal childhood wounds and transcend ourselves, as Churchill did. For ordinary people, systematically healing inner trauma, breaking free from inherent shackles, and moving toward self-actualization often becomes even more difficult.

In daily life, most of us are more like the "camel" in Nietzsche's words, silently bearing the burden of "thou shalt" — parental expectations, social norms, other people's values, constantly pressing down on our hearts. Childhood regrets intertwined with the pressures of growing up leave us struggling in anxiety, exhaustion, and self-doubt, unable to find our true selves.

This struggle often manifests vividly in our intimate relationships and family life. We often expect our partners to change because of our pain, or exert excessive control over our children in the name of "love," trying to fill the gaps in our hearts. Yet these expectations and controls not only fail to bring us closer but trap us in endless internal friction, pushing us further from true happiness.

This inner turmoil also extends to our careers and life choices. We try different career directions again and again, feeling that our lives are like a "sieve," constantly filtering through things, yet unable to find a goal that truly ignites passion.

These predicaments are not accidental. The psychologist Jung profoundly pointed out: "Until you make the unconscious conscious, it will direct your life and you will call it fate." Those emotions, beliefs, and fears hidden deep within are like invisible undercurrents, silently determining the trajectory of our lives.

*The Complete Wisdom — From Knowing Yourself to Transcending Yourself* is precisely the key, the mirror, and the lamp you have been searching for. It is not the destination, but an invitation to a journey of self-healing and transcendence.

The author, Li Zhihua, is an AI algorithm expert in Huawei's artificial intelligence security domain. With psychology as an amateur pursuit, he holds an advanced psychological counselor qualification, studied psychological healing under the Ericksonian hypnosis master, and deeply explored Zen meditation with Thich Nhat Hanh. On the boundless beach of ancient sages' wisdom, he describes himself as a curious child, bending down to pick up thought shells washed by the waves and gleaming — gathering these drops into *The Complete Wisdom*, dedicated to every reader who longs to seek the true meaning of life and the light within.

This book takes Nietzsche's "camel-lion-child" growth path as its thread, guiding people from the "camel" burdened with heavy loads and suppressed self, to the "lion" who dares to express and possesses autonomous power, and finally to the "child" who returns to innocence, openness, and infinite possibility.

On this journey, you will:

- Meet that pure inner child and learn to embrace yourself with unconditional love and acceptance;
- Accept your imperfect self amid confusion, and rediscover inner strength through awareness;
- Deeply explore the subconscious, listen to your true heart, and achieve true unity of knowledge and action;
- Through reframing and response techniques, break inherent limiting beliefs, and finally return to the perfection and freedom of your true nature.

True wisdom is not accumulated knowledge, nor rational showing off. It is more like a mirror, reflecting the shadows in our hearts; like a lamp, guiding us through the darkness of the soul until we find that peace and strength belonging to ourselves.

May you find your answer in this inward journey, discover the long-lost power and wholeness in your heart, and ultimately become the person you were always meant to be —

That free, pure, and complete self,

That person who, even after wandering half a lifetime, still returns as a youth!

## Who This Book Is For

### Who Is This Book For? Starting with a Story About Cats...

One day, my wife sighed: "There are too many cats at home (three big ones and four kittens), and I have to scoop litter every day. If only I could teach them to use the toilet."

I half-jokingly replied: "Actually, I'd rather teach them to become invisible."

She paused and asked me why.

I said seriously: "That way, when I want to play with them, they appear; when I'm annoyed, they automatically disappear. How convenient!"

Jokes aside, there is a truth behind these words: we often hope that our children, partners, colleagues, and even our pets will "go along with our wishes."

But the reality is — cats neither use the toilet nor become invisible.

Children have their own pace of growth, partners cannot read your mind, and the workplace will not treat you gently because of your grievances. So we become anxious, angry, exhausted, and self-doubting...

But the real question is not "why don't they listen," but — **"have I taken care of myself first?"**

This book does not teach you how to change the world, but accompanies you in finding that "true self" that has been neglected for so long.

### Category 1: Parents Who Want to Repair the Parent-Child Relationship — "It's for your own good," so why does it push the child further away?

Have you ever experienced a conversation like this?

> You: "Have you finished your homework? Stop playing with your phone all the time!"
> Child: "I know, stop nagging!"
>
> — The door slams shut, and your heart sinks with a thud.

The more you urge, the slower they get; the more you control, the more rebellious they become.

A lady once confided in me: "I was yelled at by my mother as a child, and now I can't help but yell at my child too. I can't control it at all."

In fact, it's not that you don't love your child, but you're afraid they'll lose at the starting line, afraid they'll take the wrong path, afraid that you're "not doing enough."

But why does that phrase "I'm doing this for your own good" push the child further and further away?

Maria Montessori once pointed out: "Children are not clay to be molded, but seeds that will sprout on their own."

Children are like sunflowers in a garden — as long as parents provide sunshine and rain, they will naturally strive to grow toward their own sun.

Yet in reality, we always want to "teach" children how to grow, forgetting that — children inherently have the power to grow toward the sun.

*The Complete Wisdom* has a chapter titled "Inner Child Healing." It doesn't teach you "how to manage children," but first leads you to see — the child within your own heart who was never properly loved.

When we first learn to soothe and heal our own inner child, only then do we have the strength to gently embrace our children.

### Category 2: People Who Want to Improve Their Partner Relationship — "You should understand me" is the most hurtful expectation

Perhaps you've had moments like these —

- You frown, hoping he'll immediately understand that you want to be hugged;
- He plays a game, and you feel he's deliberately ignoring you.
- You ask: "Do you not love me anymore?"
- He replies: "You're being unreasonable again."

One desperately sending signals, the other completely offline — this isn't the cold war, but an "emotional system disconnect."

And then there are those "performing a one-man show in marriage" — "Ten years of marriage, we're like roommates, too lazy to even argue."

In relationships, we are constantly judging our partners: "You're not considerate enough," "You're not ambitious enough," "You've changed."

We have all treated love as salvation and taking, thinking that the more we sacrifice for the other person, the more stable the relationship would be. Slowly we realized that true intimacy is not about becoming someone's accessory, nor desperately changing the other person.

It is about first liberating yourself from the role of "the sacrificer" — **only two complete people can illuminate each other.**

When we no longer rely on our partner to prove our worth, we encounter a deeper sense of happiness.

"Who are you?" "What are you good at?" "What is the thing that makes your eyes light up?"

**You are not a "problem teenager," you just haven't been truly "seen."**

Your confusion, anxiety, binge eating, and gaming addiction... are not degeneration, but a cry for help.

This book is your companion, helping you walk out of confusion and anxiety, and find your own light.

### Category 5: People Who Want to Escape Psychological Difficulties and Heal Themselves

Perhaps you're not in the above "relationship crises," yet often feel: living is so exhausting. You may be experiencing —

- **Mental internal friction**: Countless self-debates play out in your mind every day, bearing the shackles of "thou shalt" and "thou must," crushed by parental expectations and social standards.
- **Trapped by fear**: Clearly knowing there's nothing in the room, yet still afraid of the dark, afraid of things emerging from "nothingness."
- **Invisible depression**: Everything seems normal, yet your heart feels empty, lacking real emotional experience, even feeling that life has no meaning.
- **Sleep difficulties**: The more you want to sleep, the more you can't; waking up from dreams, haunted by nightmares, with nowhere to release daytime anger and grievances, only suppressing them in your heart.
- **Limiting beliefs**: About money, about ability, about happiness — you're trapped by countless "I can't," "I don't deserve," "I can't do it."
- **Unfinished goodbyes**: The death of a loved one, the rupture of a relationship, leaving a knot in your heart — grief, guilt, regret, never truly let go.
- **Shadow troubles**: You are afraid to face the "dark side" within yourself, so you desperately suppress anger, desire, and wildness, thus losing vitality.
- **Psychological fragments**: Some even feel like they've split into several "personalities," emotions unpredictable, each voice competing for dominance, becoming increasingly lost.
- **Compulsions and addictions**: Some are trapped by洁癖 (obsessive cleanliness), some by internet addiction, emotional addiction, substance addiction — it's not your fault, but your inner child crying out in extreme ways.

The healing process is not about driving these parts away, but learning to dialogue with them, reconcile with them, and gradually integrate them into a complete self.

> When shadows are embraced, they transform into strength;
> When unfinished goodbyes are allowed, they become blessings;
> When limiting beliefs are broken, you can truly be free.

## Preface

This is a journey of the soul, a pilgrimage from the noisy world toward the depths of the heart.

*The Complete Wisdom — From Knowing Yourself to Transcending Yourself* is a book about mental strength. It is not cold dogma, nor lofty preaching, but a trickling spring surging from the heart, like an oasis in the desert, inviting you to gaze at the cracks within, soothe unhealed wounds, and ultimately transcend the limited self to touch a purer, more complete you — a free soul, a reshaper of destiny.

This book is dedicated to those rebellious souls — who have left their homes, like lone wolves walking in the wilderness, turning their backs on the clamor of the world; to those lost travelers — who search for the meaning of life in the thick fog of existence; to those seekers — who ask "Who am I?" and try to break free from constraints to find their long-lost selves.

I am also one of them. On this inward journey, I too ask "Who am I?", and the answer may lie hidden in the silent dialogue between one's true nature and the Creator, waiting to be discovered.

What is wisdom? It is not accumulated knowledge, nor rational showmanship. It is more like a mirror, reflecting the shadows and light within us; like a lamp, guiding us through the darkness of the soul until we find that peace and strength belonging to ourselves.

> As Jung said: "Intellect has its value within its own domain, yet once it crosses the boundary, it becomes illusion and deception."

When reason trembles helplessly in the torrent of emotion, mental strength becomes our deepest reliance — it transcends logic and reaches directly into the boundlessness of the self.

This book will lead you into a journey of self-awakening and transcendence. You will meet that wounded inner child and learn to embrace him with unconditional love and acceptance; you will learn to accept your imperfect self amid confusion and rediscover inner strength through awareness; you will explore dreams and meditation, listen to the whispers of the subconscious, and ultimately break the beliefs that limit you, returning to the perfection of your true nature.

True seekers never follow blindly. They long for awakening, dare to question, and are as sharp as falcons. Those who have attained the Way, however, can embrace every path that benefits self-growth. This book is not the destination, but a key that will open the door to the depths of your heart. It does not promise quick answers, but invites you to walk with me — to face, to heal, and to transcend.

May you find your own answers on this inward journey.

May you find true peace and joy when the clamor finally subsides.

**1. What Is Wisdom?**

Wisdom is a complex and profound concept. In the book *The Repository of Wisdom*, wisdom is defined as the ability to see through the essence of things, possess a long-term vision, and demonstrate problem-solving abilities in adversity. Those who can persist in their goals and keep moving forward show strong willpower, not easily shaken. This ability to "meet challenges head-on" is one manifestation of wisdom. They rely not on external techniques, but on inner strength — a calm yet resilient mental power.

This mental power brings to mind the words from the *Tao Te Ching*: "Knowing others is intelligence; knowing yourself is true wisdom." This is perhaps the first step of wisdom — shifting from external observation to internal awareness.

**The Face of Wisdom** — Plato, the Stoics, Buddhist "Prajna," the Book of Proverbs, and psychologists Baltes and Sternberg, all depict the multifaceted nature of wisdom in their own ways.

**Self-Knowledge and Transcendence** — "He who conquers others is strong; he who conquers himself is mighty." True strength is conquering oneself, facing one's fears, and loosening the shackles of past imprints in the subconscious.

**2. The Journey of Self-Transcendence: From Camel to Child**

Nietzsche's "Three Metamorphoses of the Spirit": **Camel** (the burden of "thou shalt") → **Lion** (the awakening of "I will") → **Child** (the fulfillment of "I am"). This book takes this as its framework, leading readers from the "camel" toward the "child."

From Left-Behind Child to Tech Expert: My Personal Growth Journey

Hello everyone, I am a child born in 1986 in a small mountain village in Nanchong, Sichuan.

What I want to share today is not a comeback success story, but a real memory of an ordinary person finding direction amidst difficulties.

The most vivid image in my memory happened when I was two or three years old.

That day, my mother went to the market. The family had just bought a few chicks — in that era, this was the family's only hope: when they grew up, their eggs could be exchanged for money, and selling them could pay off some debt.

But when she returned, the chicks had all been carried away by weasels.

She sat on a small stool in the room, holding her head, crying loudly.

That image, I still remember to this day.

Looking back, I now understand: poverty is a force that can make people collapse.

My father was a typical "extrovert" — outgoing, couldn't stay home, always wanted to go out and make his way. He said he was going out to "do business." But speaking of "doing business," he really wasn't cut out for it.

For example, around 2010, he acted as an agent for a type of instant water heater in Wenzhou, the kind you attach to a faucet and it produces hot water when plugged in.

He bought hundreds of units in one go, but in the end only sold a few dozen, several of which he gave away for free to relatives and friends.

You know what? After helping others install them for free, he was too embarrassed to charge money. How could you possibly make money like that?

In my childhood, my father was absent.

Farm work was almost entirely carried by my mother alone. Later, the family was deeply in debt, and my parents had to go out to work to pay it back, leaving my brother and me at my grandmother's house — we became "left-behind children."

What I remember most vividly is the day they left.

I wanted to go with them, crying and begging them to take me.

As a result, my eldest uncle locked me in the room.

That feeling of "crying to heaven with no response" — the inner despair and powerlessness — is still fresh in my memory.

The only thing I could do was push over the only basket of potatoes left in the room to vent my frustration.
